# Integration authorization contract

<!-- integration-authorization-contract:v1 -->

Status: issue #9 and issue #4 runtime contract. The record itself is already specified across `01-core-flows.md` §7, `02-architecture.md` §7 and `03-technical-plan.md`; what was missing is this document, a closed schema and a validator. Panel round 1 found that absence: the only token that authorizes the one irreversible step had rules in prose and nothing that could check them.

## 1. Authority

A signed `IntegrationAuthorizationRecord` for the exact run and candidate is always required for an Epic's target CAS, as §5 and `02-architecture.md` state: `integrateApproved` refuses the CAS without one on every path. One mechanism makes it (ADR-096):

1. **Composed before the question.** The host composes the record for the exact staging identity (`composeAuthorization` in `src/host/staging-acceptance.mjs`): project, Epic and scope (`epic:<epic-id>`), target ref, the one transition from the recorded base to the commit that lands, the accepted tree and the applied commits in order come from the staging state; the controlling anchor digest and the authority projection, dependency and intent head hashes come from the identity, which binds them; the record id, run id, integration plan and classifier proof digests, the head of its scope's authorization chain it chains from (§3) and the expiry are what the plan names. It is `active`, `issued_by: user_decision_record`, and names no decision yet.
2. **Signed by the user, at the stop.** The user's `UserDecisionRecord` signs the record's payload — every field but the two that name the decision (`authorizationPayloadHash`) — with the staging identity as its subject. An Epic stops at `human` before its target branch moves: the decision packet presents the record field by field, and its `accept` option signs that payload rather than the answer object (`signed_payload_hash`, `docs/contracts/human-decision.md` §3); the request schema carries the record (`integration_authorization`, the closed shape of this schema without the two decision fields). The trusted client renders the record whose `authorizationPayloadHash` it signs, field by field, and nothing else under that option (implementation: #35 for the packet, #4 for the client). **v1 has one acceptance authority** (ADR-103): the user's signature at `accept_staging`, over the exact identity the packet presents after aggregate verification. Nothing skips the stop: every edge into `integrate_staging` or `deliver_staging` leaves `accept_staging` under a person's guard (`t_556`, `t_557`) or is that step's own retry (`t_563`, `t_573`), and neither step is where a run starts; the identity binds the aggregate record, so it exists only after aggregate verification, and nothing can be signed for an Epic before its staging does (R7-15); no step between `aggregate_verify` and the stop presents it for a signature. A pinned auto-policy is held to the same binding — `autoPolicyAcceptance` in `src/host/staging-acceptance.mjs`: its pin, the debt it tolerates, and this record, signed by the user for the exact identity, against which the policy only checks the produced identity (ADR-088) — so under v1 an auto-policy adds no autonomy: it can only remove the wait after the person has signed that exact identity, and no v1 graph edge reaches it. #9's criterion that an auto-integration policy is bound to the exact staging identity therefore reads under v1 as: every acceptance, a policy's included, carries the user's signature over the exact staging identity. An unattended acceptance — a policy that accepts without the person at the stop — needs a different binding: something the person can sign before the identity exists, and a narrow exception, for that path alone, to this section's rule that no policy issues the record. Designing it is #28's own post-v1 design work (Autobuild, `planned_after_v1`; its run contract's `approved_auto_policy`), which a successor panel reviews; `autoPolicyAcceptance` cannot admit it (review of `99fd30b`, M1).
3. **Completed and checked by one rule.** The verified decision completes the record with its `user_decision_record_id` and `user_decision_record_hash`, and the host checks the completed record by one rule (`assertAuthorized`, reached from `acceptanceFromDecision` and from the auto-policy's binding, `autoPolicyAcceptance`; `docs/contracts/epic-staging.md` §5, ADR-091): the decision verifies, signed exactly this record about exactly this identity, and the record is the one composed for it. The acceptance names the record by `integration_authorization_id` and `integration_authorization_sha256`, and the CAS admission refuses an acceptance without that reference or asked under a record with another id (`acceptance_missing`), and the named record revoked, replaced or rewritten since, or no longer about this staging state, terminal or expired (`acceptance_stale`, §5; `acceptanceErrors`).

The host composes and verifies; it does not issue, store or chain. autoskd stores the record in its scope's chain, under the one `integration_authorization_head` it keeps per project for integrity (ADR-023, §5), and `integrateApproved` checks, before the CAS, what the host cannot read: that the record resolved by scope and id is the one named and is its scope's current record, chained from the one before it, and that the heads and the controlling anchor digest it names are the ones in force — which the scope's own acceptance does not move (§3). Until the ADR-023 verifier exists the host refuses every record, the person's and the auto-policy binding's alike.

Nothing skips the stop, and nothing replaces the record. A project policy may not issue one — policy covers scheduling and reversible non-product scope, and moving a user's branch is neither. A model may prepare the packet and recommend it; the authority is a signed `UserDecisionRecord`, and a record without one authorizes nothing.

## 2. Boundaries

This contract decides what the record binds, when it stops authorizing, and that it authorizes exactly one ref transition, so no partial integration is ever left for a later record to continue. It does not decide how the CAS itself is performed (`docs/contracts/epic-staging.md` §6), who may move the branch at all (`docs/contracts/delivery-profile.md` §6a), or how the decision reaches the operator (`docs/contracts/human-decision.md`).

## 3. What the record binds

The closed JSON Schema is `resources/integration-authorization/integration-authorization.schema.json`.

- `record_id` and `scope_id` — the record is resolved by scope **and** id, so a record from another scope is not found by luck;
- `project_root_sha256`, `epic_id` (or `null` with `quick_task_id` for a Quick run) and `run_id`;
- `target_ref` and `initial_target_oid` — the branch and where it was when the record was signed: the recorded base the one CAS expects to find;
- `ref_transition` — the one movement the record authorizes, with `from_oid` equal to `initial_target_oid` and `to_oid` the commit that carries the accepted identity: an Epic's accepted staging commit, built on the verified `planning_head` that descends from the recorded base (or, after a re-stage, one receipted planning replay commit on the new `recorded_target_base`), so the planning commits move with it (the `merge` and `rebase` modes), or its squash commit — one commit whose tree is the accepted staging tree and whose only parent is the recorded base, built by a deterministic recipe fixed before the record is signed (the `squash` mode; `to_oid` equals the squash commit OID the acceptance names as `target_commit_oid`, beside its `target_commit_recipe_sha256` over message, author, committer, timestamps, tree and parent, so the person accepts the exact commit that lands) — or a Quick run's integration commit, which the adapter builds from a deterministic recipe — recorded base, reviewed candidate, approved tree — fixed before the record is signed, the way `commit_on_pass` fixes its expected commit OID, so the OID the record names is the one the CAS writes. A transition, not a permission to reach a state by any route (§4);
- `ordered_ticket_commit_oids` — the reviewed commits that result is made of, in the order they were applied: the Tickets whose approved deltas the Epic's staging carries, or the Quick run's one reviewed candidate, and a Quick record naming any other number is refused;
- `final_tree_oid` — what the branch is expected to hold afterwards;
- `integration_plan_hash`, `controlling_anchor_digest`, `classifier_proof_hash`;
- `relevant_authority_projection_hash`, `dependency_head_hash`, `intent_head_hash` — the heads the signature was made against. **An acceptance does not move what it accepts** (ADR-103): the scope's own integration acceptance never enters the scope's relevant authority projection, its dependency head or its intent head, so it never moves the controlling anchor digest either. It is a class, not one decision (review of `99fd30b`, L3): every answer to any acceptance packet of the scope — `accept` or `refuse`, to any question, re-asks included — and every `IntegrationAuthorizationRecord` of the scope. autoskd commits a decision before the record it completes exists (`01-core-flows.md` §2), so it keys the class by the kind of request the decision answers, one rule for both kinds of scope (ADR-112) — an Epic's packet parked with `acceptance_missing` at `accept_staging` for an Epic's scope (`epic:<epic-id>`), or a Quick run's packet parked with `integration_authorization_required` at `accept` for a Quick run's scope (`quick:<task-id>`) — never by its payload; committing it moves the global authority and nonce heads, which `integrateApproved` reconciles for integrity only, as it does an unrelated append (`03-technical-plan.md` §4, `authorityGuard`), and storing the record moves `integration_authorization_head` (§5), never a head the record names. `integrateApproved` therefore compares the named heads with the heads in force under that exclusion. Every other decision is outside the class and counts: anything that enters the scope's relevant projection, its dependency head or its intent head after the question — a waiver, a policy issued or revoked, an authority record the scope depends on added or superseded, a correction, and a later decision revoking or replacing an acceptance decision or record — still makes the acceptance stale (§5); revoking or replacing the record is additionally enforced by the scope's chain (§5);
- `previous_scope_authorization_hash` — the chain of the record's scope: the previous record of the same `scope_id`, named by its digest, or `null` for the scope's first, so a record cannot be inserted behind one that already exists in its scope. It is the scope's own chain and not the project's `integration_authorization_head`, which the field's earlier name suggested (ADR-112). The chain is per scope, under one `integration_authorization_head` per project kept for integrity (§5, ADR-103);
- `expires_at` and `terminal_disposition`;
- `user_decision_record_id` and `user_decision_record_hash`.

Every one of those is load-bearing: each names something that, if it changed, would make the authorization about a different integration.

Every OID the record names — `initial_target_oid`, both ends of `ref_transition`, `ordered_ticket_commit_oids` and `final_tree_oid` — is a full OID of the project repository's object format: 40 lowercase hex characters for sha1, 64 for sha256, and one format for the whole record, because a repository has one (ADR-098). The schema admits either and refuses a record that mixes them, as the staging record's schema does, so a SHA-256 repository reaches a schema-valid record the same way a SHA-1 one does.

Who checks each field before the CAS. The host (§1), when the acceptance is made and again at the CAS admission against the staging state: the project, Epic, scope and target, `initial_target_oid` and `ref_transition` against the recorded base and the commit that lands, `final_tree_oid` against the accepted tree, `ordered_ticket_commit_oids` against the staging receipts in order, the controlling anchor digest and the three heads against the ones the identity binds, `terminal_disposition` and `expires_at`, and the decision behind it. The host cannot check that those heads and `previous_scope_authorization_hash` are the daemon's current ones, nor recompute `integration_plan_hash` or `classifier_proof_hash` (no calculator exists yet, ADR-091): the user signs them as presented, and `integrateApproved` asserts the heads — in force under the exclusion above — and the scope's chain (§5). No host code computes a head: the host takes the three heads from its caller (`acceptanceFacts`) and the head the record chains from as the plan names it (`composeAuthorization`); the daemon computes them (#4) and `integrateApproved` compares them (#9).

## 4. Expiry is not a formality

An expired record authorizes nothing. There is no partial integration for it to strand, because the branch moves by **one** ref transition: an Epic's from the recorded target base to the accepted staging commit or, under `squash`, the squash commit the acceptance names, by the one CAS after aggregate PASS and acceptance (`docs/contracts/epic-staging.md` §6), and a Quick run's from its base to the commit that integrates its reviewed candidate. So the record names that one transition and nothing else: no ordered plan, no position inside one, no receipt for a part already done. The schema has no field that could say otherwise, and a record shaped for the per-Ticket order this contract was first written for — several transitions, a start index, a receipt for a completed part — is refused by the schema rather than read.

A record that expires before the CAS leaves the branch where it was and the acceptance it backed stale: the Epic returns to `accept_staging`, and a new record binds the same recorded base and the same accepted identity or it binds nothing. After the CAS there is nothing left to authorize. A record that authorized moving `A → B` is still not authority for moving `A → C` unless somebody signed that.

## 5. Fail-closed, and what may not substitute

A missing, changed, shortened or head-mismatched record refuses, and the target ref is not read again and not moved. For a Quick run the refusal parks at `accept` with `integration_authorization_required`. An Epic never parks with that code: its acceptance is what the record backs, so a record that is missing when acceptance is asked for is `acceptance_missing` at `accept_staging`, and one that has expired, been revoked or replaced, or no longer matches its scope's chain under `integration_authorization_head` by the time `integrate_staging` would run the CAS is `acceptance_stale` there, resumed at `accept_staging` for a new acceptance. Recovery restores exact committed bytes; it never reconstructs a record from what the operation appears to have been doing. The host's check of the record behind an acceptance (`docs/contracts/epic-staging.md` §5), for either kind of acceptance, sees only the copy it is handed, so revocation or replacement after that point is enforced here, by `integrateApproved` against its scope's chain under `integration_authorization_head`, and not by the host; the host's CAS admission refuses a record that is terminal or expired in the copy it is handed.

**One head for integrity, one chain per scope** (ADR-103). autoskd keeps one `integration_authorization_head` per project: every record it stores advances it, and `integrateApproved` reconciles the stored records against it for integrity, so a record deleted, rewritten or rolled back is found (anti-rollback), as it reconciles the global authority head for an authority record (`03-technical-plan.md` §4). The chain a record names is its scope's: `previous_scope_authorization_hash` is the previous record of the same `scope_id`, and two checks hold it. When a record is stored, autoskd requires its `previous_scope_authorization_hash` to be its scope's head just before (the store-time check, which `recordRefusals` models over the worked examples). At the CAS, `integrateApproved` requires its scope's head to be the named record's own digest: the record the acceptance names is that scope's current record, chained from the one before it (review of `99fd30b`, L4). Another Epic's or a Quick run's record, stored between this record's question and its CAS, moves the global head and its own scope's head and never this one's, so it does not stale this acceptance and asks the person for nothing; a record revoked or replaced in this scope does stale it. Anti-rollback holds per scope because the global head commits to every scope's chain: rolling back one scope's records breaks it. A chain per project would couple every Epic to every other — any record stored anywhere between a question and its CAS would make the acceptance stale and ask the person again — a coupling the one CAS, which already serializes Epics on a target (ADR-099), does not need.

`integration-state/<operation-id>.json` stores the CAS operation and its outcome. It is not authority and may not stand in for the record: operation state is what happened, and authorization is what was permitted, and inferring the second from the first is how an interrupted CAS would authorize its own retry.

## 6. Refusal classes

Closed set: `integration_authorization_required`, `integration_authorization_expired`, `integration_authorization_scope_mismatch`, `integration_authorization_prefix_mismatch`, `integration_authorization_head_mismatch`, `integration_authorization_policy_issued`, `integration_authorization_terminal`.

These classes name why a record refuses; the stop they cause is named by the flow that meets it. A Quick run parks with `integration_authorization_required` at `accept`. An Epic parks with the graph's `acceptance_missing` or `acceptance_stale` (§5), and the host raises those for it — no host code raises an `integration_authorization_*` class, so no Epic stop has two names. The correspondence, for an Epic: required, or `policy_issued` (no signed decision, or one that did not sign this record) → `acceptance_missing`; `scope_mismatch`, `prefix_mismatch` (another project, Epic, target, transition or tree), `expired`, `terminal` → `acceptance_stale`; `head_mismatch` → `acceptance_stale` at `integrate_staging`, raised by `integrateApproved`, since only the daemon holds the head. `integration_authorization_required` is declared `none` in the refusal vocabulary: its evaluator, the Quick `accept` step, is not implemented, and `recordRefusals` in `scripts/validate-integration-authorization.mjs` computes the classes over the worked examples only.

`integration_authorization_prefix_mismatch` keeps the name it had under the per-Ticket order and refuses one case now: the branch is not where the record's one transition starts, so the record is about a branch state other than the one it would act on, and it does not follow the branch there.

## 7. What this contract decides, and what it defers

Decided: what the record binds, how it comes into being (composed before the question, signed by the user at the stop, completed by the verified decision, named by the acceptance), that v1 has that one acceptance authority, under which a pinned auto-policy is held to the same binding, adds no autonomy and is reached by no v1 path (§1, ADR-103), that an acceptance does not move the heads it names, an Epic's and a Quick run's alike (§3, ADR-112), that the chain is per scope under one head kept for integrity (§5), that it authorizes exactly one ref transition from the recorded base — so no partial CAS exists for a later record to continue, and the schema has no field for one — that policy cannot issue it, that expiry ends it, and that operation state is not authority.

Deferred, and named: the signer itself and the trusted client that displays the challenge — both are daemon-side and are covered by ADR-023; storing and chaining the record and the head check in `integrateApproved` (#4, #9), computing the heads under §3's exclusion, recognizing its class at commit by either kind of request (#4) and comparing them and the scope's chain under §5 (#9); the binding an unattended acceptance would need — something signable before the identity exists, and the narrow exception to §1's rule that no policy issues the record — which is #28's own post-v1 design work, reviewed by a successor panel (`planned_after_v1`); the trusted client that renders the presented record the user signs (#4, #35); composing and checking a Quick run's record at `accept`, and comparing its heads under the same exclusion (the Quick path, #9); the calculators of `integration_plan_hash` and `classifier_proof_hash`.
