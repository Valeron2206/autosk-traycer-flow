# Integration authorization contract

<!-- integration-authorization-contract:v1 -->

Status: issue #9 and issue #4 runtime contract. The record itself is already specified across `01-core-flows.md` §7, `02-architecture.md` §7 and `03-technical-plan.md`; what was missing is this document, a closed schema and a validator. Panel round 1 found that absence: the only token that may skip the human stop before the one irreversible step had rules in prose and nothing that could check them.

## 1. Authority

A signed `IntegrationAuthorizationRecord` for the exact run and candidate is always required for an Epic's target CAS, as §5 and `02-architecture.md` state: `integrateApproved` refuses the CAS without one on every path. One mechanism makes it on both paths (ADR-096):

1. **Composed before the question.** The host composes the record for the exact staging identity (`composeAuthorization` in `src/host/staging-acceptance.mjs`): project, Epic and scope (`epic:<epic-id>`), target ref, the one transition from the recorded base to the commit that lands, the accepted tree and the applied commits in order come from the staging state; the controlling anchor digest and the authority projection, dependency and intent head hashes come from the identity, which binds them; the record id, run id, integration plan and classifier proof digests, the authorization head it chains from and the expiry are what the plan names. It is `active`, `issued_by: user_decision_record`, and names no decision yet.
2. **Signed by the user.** The user's `UserDecisionRecord` signs the record's payload — every field but the two that name the decision (`authorizationPayloadHash`) — with the staging identity as its subject. By default an Epic stops at `human` before its target branch moves: the decision packet presents the record field by field, and its `accept` option signs that payload rather than the answer object (`signed_payload_hash`, `docs/contracts/human-decision.md` §3); the request schema carries the record (`integration_authorization`, the closed shape of this schema without the two decision fields). The trusted client renders the record whose `authorizationPayloadHash` it signs, field by field, and nothing else under that option (implementation: #35 for the packet, #4 for the client). Or the user signed the same record for a pinned auto-policy before that step, and that record is the one thing that may skip the stop: the policy then only checks that the produced identity is the one the record names (ADR-088). "In advance" means before the acceptance step for that exact identity; the identity binds the aggregate record, so it exists only after aggregate verification, and nothing can be signed for an Epic before its staging does.
3. **Completed and checked by one rule.** The verified decision completes the record with its `user_decision_record_id` and `user_decision_record_hash`, and the host checks the completed record the same way on both paths (`assertAuthorized`, reached from `acceptanceFromDecision` and `autoPolicyAcceptance`, `docs/contracts/epic-staging.md` §5, ADR-091): the decision verifies, signed exactly this record about exactly this identity, and the record is the one composed for it. The acceptance names the record by `integration_authorization_id` and `integration_authorization_sha256`, and the CAS admission refuses an acceptance without that reference or asked under a record with another id (`acceptance_missing`), and the named record revoked, replaced or rewritten since, or no longer about this staging state, terminal or expired (`acceptance_stale`, §5; `acceptanceErrors`).

The host composes and verifies; it does not issue, store or chain. autoskd stores the record under `integration_authorization_head` (ADR-023), and `integrateApproved` checks, before the CAS, what the host cannot read: that the record resolved by scope and id is the one named, that it chains from the current head, and that the heads and the controlling anchor digest it names are the ones in force. Until the ADR-023 verifier exists the host refuses every record on both paths.

Nothing else may skip the stop, and nothing replaces the record. A project policy may not issue one — policy covers scheduling and reversible non-product scope, and moving a user's branch is neither. A model may prepare the packet and recommend it; the authority is a signed `UserDecisionRecord`, and a record without one authorizes nothing.

## 2. Boundaries

This contract decides what the record binds, when it stops authorizing, and that it authorizes exactly one ref transition, so no partial integration is ever left for a later record to continue. It does not decide how the CAS itself is performed (`docs/contracts/epic-staging.md` §6), who may move the branch at all (`docs/contracts/delivery-profile.md` §6a), or how the decision reaches the operator (`docs/contracts/human-decision.md`).

## 3. What the record binds

The closed JSON Schema is `resources/integration-authorization/integration-authorization.schema.json`.

- `record_id` and `scope_id` — the record is resolved by scope **and** id, so a record from another scope is not found by luck;
- `project_root_sha256`, `epic_id` (or `null` with `quick_task_id` for a Quick run) and `run_id`;
- `target_ref` and `initial_target_oid` — the branch and where it was when the record was signed: the recorded base the one CAS expects to find;
- `ref_transition` — the one movement the record authorizes, with `from_oid` equal to `initial_target_oid` and `to_oid` the commit that carries the accepted identity: an Epic's accepted staging commit, built on the verified `planning_head` that descends from the recorded base (or, after a re-stage, one receipted planning replay commit on the new `recorded_target_base`), so the planning commits move with it (the `merge` and `rebase` modes), or its squash commit — one commit whose tree is the accepted staging tree and whose only parent is the recorded base, built by a deterministic recipe fixed before the record is signed (the `squash` mode; `to_oid` equals the squash commit OID the acceptance names as `target_commit_oid`, beside its `target_commit_recipe_sha256` over message, author, committer, timestamps, tree and parent, so the person accepts the exact commit that lands) — or a Quick run's integration commit, which the adapter builds from a deterministic recipe — recorded base, reviewed candidate, approved tree — fixed before the record is signed, the way `commit_on_pass` fixes its expected commit OID, so the OID the record names is the one the CAS writes. A transition, not a permission to reach a state by any route (§4);
- `ordered_ticket_commit_oids` — the reviewed commits that result is made of, in the order they were applied: the Tickets whose approved deltas the Epic's staging carries, or the Quick run's one reviewed candidate, and a Quick record naming any other number is refused;
- `final_tree_oid` — what the branch is expected to hold afterwards;
- `integration_plan_hash`, `controlling_anchor_digest`, `classifier_proof_hash`;
- `relevant_authority_projection_hash`, `dependency_head_hash`, `intent_head_hash` — the heads the signature was made against;
- `previous_authorization_head_hash` — the chain, so a record cannot be inserted behind one that already exists;
- `expires_at` and `terminal_disposition`;
- `user_decision_record_id` and `user_decision_record_hash`.

Every one of those is load-bearing: each names something that, if it changed, would make the authorization about a different integration.

Who checks each field before the CAS. The host (§1), when the acceptance is made and again at the CAS admission against the staging state: the project, Epic, scope and target, `initial_target_oid` and `ref_transition` against the recorded base and the commit that lands, `final_tree_oid` against the accepted tree, `ordered_ticket_commit_oids` against the staging receipts in order, the controlling anchor digest and the three heads against the ones the identity binds, `terminal_disposition` and `expires_at`, and the decision behind it. The host cannot check that those heads and `previous_authorization_head_hash` are the daemon's current ones, nor recompute `integration_plan_hash` or `classifier_proof_hash` (no calculator exists yet, ADR-091): the user signs them as presented, and `integrateApproved` asserts the heads and the chain.

## 4. Expiry is not a formality

An expired record authorizes nothing. There is no partial integration for it to strand, because the branch moves by **one** ref transition: an Epic's from the recorded target base to the accepted staging commit or, under `squash`, the squash commit the acceptance names, by the one CAS after aggregate PASS and acceptance (`docs/contracts/epic-staging.md` §6), and a Quick run's from its base to the commit that integrates its reviewed candidate. So the record names that one transition and nothing else: no ordered plan, no position inside one, no receipt for a part already done. The schema has no field that could say otherwise, and a record shaped for the per-Ticket order this contract was first written for — several transitions, a start index, a receipt for a completed part — is refused by the schema rather than read.

A record that expires before the CAS leaves the branch where it was and the acceptance it backed stale: the Epic returns to `accept_staging`, and a new record binds the same recorded base and the same accepted identity or it binds nothing. After the CAS there is nothing left to authorize. A record that authorized moving `A → B` is still not authority for moving `A → C` unless somebody signed that.

## 5. Fail-closed, and what may not substitute

A missing, changed, shortened or head-mismatched record refuses, and the target ref is not read again and not moved. For a Quick run the refusal parks at `accept` with `integration_authorization_required`. An Epic never parks with that code: its acceptance is what the record backs, so a record that is missing when acceptance is asked for is `acceptance_missing` at `accept_staging`, and one that has expired, been revoked or replaced, or no longer matches `integration_authorization_head` by the time `integrate_staging` would run the CAS is `acceptance_stale` there, resumed at `accept_staging` for a new acceptance. Recovery restores exact committed bytes; it never reconstructs a record from what the operation appears to have been doing. The host's check of the record behind an acceptance (`docs/contracts/epic-staging.md` §5), on either path, sees only the copy it is handed, so revocation or replacement after that point is enforced here, by `integrateApproved` against `integration_authorization_head`, and not by the host; the host's CAS admission refuses a record that is terminal or expired in the copy it is handed.

`integration-state/<operation-id>.json` stores the CAS operation and its outcome. It is not authority and may not stand in for the record: operation state is what happened, and authorization is what was permitted, and inferring the second from the first is how an interrupted CAS would authorize its own retry.

## 6. Refusal classes

Closed set: `integration_authorization_required`, `integration_authorization_expired`, `integration_authorization_scope_mismatch`, `integration_authorization_prefix_mismatch`, `integration_authorization_head_mismatch`, `integration_authorization_policy_issued`, `integration_authorization_terminal`.

These classes name why a record refuses; the stop they cause is named by the flow that meets it. A Quick run parks with `integration_authorization_required` at `accept`. An Epic parks with the graph's `acceptance_missing` or `acceptance_stale` (§5), and the host raises those for it — no host code raises an `integration_authorization_*` class, so no Epic stop has two names. The correspondence, for an Epic: required, or `policy_issued` (no signed decision, or one that did not sign this record) → `acceptance_missing`; `scope_mismatch`, `prefix_mismatch` (another project, Epic, target, transition or tree), `expired`, `terminal` → `acceptance_stale`; `head_mismatch` → `acceptance_stale` at `integrate_staging`, raised by `integrateApproved`, since only the daemon holds the head. `integration_authorization_required` is declared `none` in the refusal vocabulary: its evaluator, the Quick `accept` step, is not implemented, and `recordRefusals` in `scripts/validate-integration-authorization.mjs` computes the classes over the worked examples only.

`integration_authorization_prefix_mismatch` keeps the name it had under the per-Ticket order and refuses one case now: the branch is not where the record's one transition starts, so the record is about a branch state other than the one it would act on, and it does not follow the branch there.

## 7. What this contract decides, and what it defers

Decided: what the record binds, how it comes into being on both Epic paths (composed before the question, signed by the user, completed by the verified decision, named by the acceptance), that it authorizes exactly one ref transition from the recorded base — so no partial CAS exists for a later record to continue, and the schema has no field for one — that policy cannot issue it, that expiry ends it, and that operation state is not authority.

Deferred, and named: the signer itself and the trusted client that displays the challenge — both are daemon-side and are covered by ADR-023; storing and chaining the record and the head check in `integrateApproved` (#4, #9); the trusted client that renders the presented record the user signs (#4, #35); composing and checking a Quick run's record at `accept` (the Quick path, #9); the calculators of `integration_plan_hash` and `classifier_proof_hash`.
